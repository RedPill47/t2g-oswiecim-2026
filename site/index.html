The trip at a glance

Six days: a night on the bus, three days in Oświęcim, a day in Kraków, a night on the bus home.

  1. Thu nightLuxembourg, by bus
  2. Fri – SunOświęcim
  3. MonKraków
  4. TueHome, early afternoon

How the Oświęcim days work

Friday to Sunday in four lines.

  • MorningsSaturday and Sunday mornings are the memorial sites, each followed by a commemoration ceremony: the main camp (Auschwitz I) and the Wall of Death on Saturday, Birkenau (Auschwitz II) and the International Monument on Sunday.
  • AfternoonsTwo slots each day, 15:00 and 16:30. You pick one option per slot. Workshops run in named rooms at the IJBS; visits leave from the Forum / Reception.
  • EveningsSaturday 20:00: study groups. Chaperones, private participants, teachers, educators and university students have a meeting in the Library at 19:30 on Friday, Saturday and Sunday.
  • Any timeCards and pens are laid out around the IJBS. Write or draw what moves you, in any language, and hang it on the line in the foyer. The Hannah Arendt exhibition is open to visit on your own.

Still not in the documents: meal times and the exact start of the morning visits. Ask your group leader.

PrepareWhat the association recommends before you go

  • Multibook in French: the Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum’s digital guide to the history of the camp. Also in English and German.
  • Ein Tag in Auschwitz: ZDF documentary (2020, German) about one day in May 1944, told from the victims’ point of view and some perpetrators’.
  • 50Faces – 50 Stories: 50 wartime biographies seen from Luxembourg – occupation, injustice, dictatorship, genocide. The same tool Yannick Frantz presents on Friday.
  • Information brochure (PDF): the association’s general brochure, 2022 edition.

Words you’ll hear

Oświęcim / Auschwitz
Oświęcim is the Polish town (about 40,000 people today). Auschwitz is its German name, used by the Nazis for the camp complex built there.
Stammlager – Auschwitz I
The original main camp, with the brick blocks. Block 10 and Block 11 (the standing cells) are part of it.
Birkenau – Auschwitz II
The much larger camp a few kilometres away, where most victims were murdered. The programme calls it the “death factory”.
IJBS
Internationale Jugendbegegnungsstätte – the International Youth Meeting Centre in Oświęcim. Every room in the planning (House of Silence, Oppenheimer, Szymanski, Przybylski, Library, Café, Forum) is here.
Zeitzeuge, 2nd / 3rd generation
A witness. The 2nd generation are children of people persecuted during the Shoah; the 3rd generation are grandchildren.
Shoah
The Hebrew word for the Holocaust, the murder of six million European Jews.
Sonderkommando
Prisoners forced to work at the gas chambers and crematoria.
Death march (Todesmarsch)
The forced evacuation of prisoners on foot in January 1945, as the Soviet army approached.
Wall of Death (mur des fusillés)
The execution wall in the courtyard between Blocks 10 and 11 of the main camp, where thousands of prisoners were shot. Saturday’s ceremony takes place here.
International Monument
The memorial to the victims at the end of the railway ramp in Birkenau, between the ruins of crematoria II and III. Sunday’s ceremony takes place here.
Kazimierz
Kraków’s old Jewish quarter, which you visit on Monday.
Language codes
L = Luxembourgish, D = German, F = French, E = English. A letter in brackets means it’s used partly.
mod.
Moderated by – the person hosting a talk.
Jonker fir Jonker
Luxembourgish for “young people for young people”: sessions led by students who did the trip before.
